Bull Analyst: # Bull Case for Intel (INTC)

Let me lay out why this is one of the most compelling turnaround stories in tech right now, and why the bears are focusing on backward-looking noise while missing the operational inflection happening in real time.

1. The core business is genuinely turning around — the numbers prove it. Forget the headline net loss for a second. Q2 2026 revenue came in at $16.13B, up 25% YoY. Gross margin expanded from 27.5% to 40.3% — that's nearly 1,300 basis points of improvement in a single year. Operating margin is solidly positive at 12.19%. Operating cash flow nearly quadrupled YoY to $7.0B, and free cash flow is now positive ($4.87B TTM) after years of cash burn. This is exactly the trajectory you want to see from a company mid-turnaround: revenue growth, margin expansion, cash generation improving, and capex moderating as the investment cycle matures. That's not speculation — that's the tape.

2. The GAAP net loss is a distraction, not a red flag. Yes, Intel posted an $11B net loss, but that was driven by a $12.5B non-cash "loss on sale of security" and impairment charges — one-off items, not operational deterioration. Strip those out and the underlying business was profitable at the operating line. Sophisticated investors know to look through these charges, and that's precisely why the stock has been re-rating — the market is pricing the real trend, not the noisy GAAP print.

3. Technology leadership is materializing, not just promised. Intel and ASML have moved High-NA EUV lithography into actual production — this is a genuine, verifiable technical milestone that puts Intel at the frontier of next-gen chipmaking (18A/14A nodes). Analysts are now citing 59% AI-driven growth in their models, with fresh $120 price targets. This isn't hype — it's a business model shift from "legacy CPU maker" to "AI-era foundry and compute platform," and the market is starting to recognize it.

4. Government backing is an underappreciated de-risking event. The U.S. government taking an equity stake in Intel isn't a red flag — it's a strategic backstop. It signals that Intel's foundry ambitions are viewed as a national security priority, effectively lowering the tail-risk of a failed foundry buildout. Few companies in this sector have that kind of implicit support.

5. Technicals confirm this isn't just retail froth. The stock rallied from $82 to $104 on rising OBV — meaning real volume, real conviction, not a low-volume speculative spike. Price is above both the 50-day ($100.13) and 200-day ($74.51) SMAs, with the 50 now above the 200 — a bullish long-term structural setup. RSI at 61.7 shows strength without being overbought. Yes, ADX is low and weekly SuperTrend hasn't flipped yet — but that just means there's room left to run once trend confirmation catches up to price action, not that the move is invalid.

Now let's address the bear concerns directly:

  • "Valuation is stretched at 52x forward P/E." Look at the PEG ratio instead — 1.36 is reasonable given the growth trajectory. Forward EPS is projected to swing from -$2.09 to +$2.04 — that's the swing the market is pricing, and it's the right thing to price given the margin and revenue trends already visible in the last two quarters.

  • "Equity eroded 23% in two quarters." That's a snapshot of transition-period charges (impairments, restructuring) working through the balance sheet — not a sign of business failure. The operating cash flow trend tells the real story, and it's improving fast.

  • "AMD reaffirmed TSMC ties — foundry customer risk." This is a single headline, not a structural loss. IFS is a multi-year process; no one expected every major fabless customer to jump ship on day one. Meanwhile the High-NA EUV milestone strengthens Intel's technical pitch to future customers.

  • "Apple distancing from Intel." This is old news about the legacy client-computing business, largely priced in years ago. It's irrelevant to the AI/foundry growth thesis driving the re-rating.
