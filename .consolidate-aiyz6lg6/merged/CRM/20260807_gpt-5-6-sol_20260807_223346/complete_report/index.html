1. CRM is still growing at a scale few software companies can match¶

  • Diluted EPS rose approximately 52.2%, aided by a roughly 10% year-over-year reduction in diluted shares.

At the supplied market price of $192.74, CRM trades at approximately:

  • 12.4x forward earnings
  • 0.78x PEG
  • 3.7x TTM revenue
  • Approximately 10.5% TTM free-cash-flow yield

Those are not premium-growth multiples. If the forward EPS estimate of $15.51 is even approximately achievable, the current valuation leaves room for meaningful upside from either earnings growth or multiple normalization.

The bear might say the forward EPS estimate is too optimistic—and that is fair. But the answer is not to ignore the estimate; it is to test what must go right. The forecast is supported by an existing profitable engine, not by an unprofitable business requiring a speculative turnaround.

3. CRM’s competitive advantage is its installed base, data, and workflow position¶

AI does not automatically destroy incumbent software companies. In many cases, AI makes high-quality proprietary data, customer context, permissions, and workflow integration more valuable.

CRM is embedded in the daily operations of large enterprises across sales, service, marketing, commerce, analytics, and customer data. That creates several advantages:

  • Switching costs are significant.
  • Customer information and historical workflow data are difficult to replicate.
  • The platform can distribute new AI capabilities to an existing customer base.
  • CRM can monetize AI inside workflows customers already use rather than convincing them to adopt an entirely new system.

The bear’s argument that AI agents could bypass traditional software is intellectually serious. But it assumes the agent will operate independently of the system containing the customer record, business rules, approvals, permissions, and transaction history. In enterprise environments, those controls matter. The more likely outcome is not that CRM disappears, but that CRM evolves from a system employees interact with into a system AI agents use on behalf of employees.

I would not rely on unsupported social-media claims about a specific $300 million–$500 million AI revenue contribution. The stronger argument is that CRM has the distribution, data, and enterprise relationships necessary to monetize AI if execution is competent.

  • Total debt increased to $41.88 billion.

  • Net debt is approximately 2.35x TTM EBITDA.
  • Net debt is approximately 1.8x TTM free cash flow.
  • CRM generated $16.55 billion of TTM free cash flow.

This is not a debt-free balance sheet, but it is also not a distressed capital structure. CRM has the earnings and cash flow to deleverage if management moderates buybacks and acquisition spending. The issue is capital-allocation discipline, not business solvency.

The latest quarter’s $6.56 billion of free cash flow was helped by unusually favorable working-capital movements, so I would not annualize it mechanically. But even after discounting that quarter, the TTM figure demonstrates substantial underlying cash-generation power.
