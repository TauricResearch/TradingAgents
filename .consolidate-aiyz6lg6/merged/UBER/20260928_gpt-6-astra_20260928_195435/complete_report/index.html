Bull Researcher¶

Bull Analyst: ## Opening bull case: UBER

My case for UBER is not that the September 28 close of $68.16 marks a bottom. The chart is bearish near term. The case is that the underlying business has become more profitable and cash-generative than the recent share-price decline suggests.

Start with the operating results. Revenue grew 18.3% in 2025 to $52.02 billion, then rose another 12.2% year over year in the June 2026 quarter. More importantly, June-quarter operating income grew 30.3% on that 12.2% revenue growth, and operating margin improved from 11.5% to 13.3%. That is the scalability I want to own: growth translating into disproportionately more operating profit. Annual free cash flow rose from $0.39 billion in 2022 to $9.76 billion in 2025.

The opportunity extends across mobility, delivery and freight. UBER’s established marketplace and brand give it a way to distribute additional services without building a separate customer-facing platform each time. The Costco delivery partnership’s reported expansion from 17 to 47 states is a concrete example of broader distribution—though I would want to see profitable order growth before assigning it a valuation premium. Autonomous vehicles could become another source of trips on UBER’s marketplace, but that is an option, not a benefit I am counting today.

The bear is right to challenge valuation. I would not pitch UBER on its 14.95× trailing P/E: tax benefits and securities gains materially inflate reported earnings. Cash flow is the better starting point, with an important caveat. The supplied quarterly statements sum to $10.12 billion of trailing free cash flow, while the vendor snapshot says $7.24 billion. Against the reported $139.22 billion market cap, that is an illustrative 5.2%–7.3% yield, not one settled figure. Reconcile the discrepancy before treating the higher yield as fact.

Nor would I wave away the balance sheet. June working capital was negative $2.34 billion, cash and short-term investments fell, and debt rose. Those are real tests of this thesis, especially if the reported Delivery Hero tender proves to require substantial funding; its terms and UBER’s exposure remain unverified. Still, the business generated $2.79 billion of free cash flow in the June quarter, and shares outstanding were down about 2.2% year over year despite stock compensation. This is not a growth story dependent solely on issuing more shares.

For scale, consider a scenario, not company guidance: if the $55.23 billion trailing revenue base grows 10%–12% over the following year and its roughly 12.1% trailing operating margin holds, revenue would reach about $60.8 billion–$61.9 billion and operating income about $7.4 billion–$7.5 billion.
