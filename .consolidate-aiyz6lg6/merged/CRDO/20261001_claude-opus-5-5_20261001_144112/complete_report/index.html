Credo's active electrical cables (AECs), optical DSPs, retimers, and SerDes IP address exactly that. Growth like that suggests real demand, not a narrative.

2. The advantage is technical and structural. - Credo's SerDes heritage gives it a power-efficiency edge, and power is the scarcest resource in AI data centers. - AECs have no lasers, so they typically use less power and fail less often than optical modules at short reach. That matters when one failed link can stall an expensive training run. - It is a fabless model with non-GAAP gross margins in the mid-to-high 60s, so incremental revenue drops through at high rates.

3. Design wins are sticky. Hyperscaler qualification cycles are long. Once Credo is designed into a platform, it tends to stay through that generation, and each new speed transition (800G to 1.6T and beyond) creates another upgrade cycle.

Likely bear points and my responses¶

  • Customer concentration. This is the most legitimate risk. But concentration is endemic to AI semiconductors, and Credo's base had broadened to several large customers rather than one. Amazon's warrants vest with purchase volume, so they signal committed demand, though the dilution is real.
  • Valuation. The multiple is premium, and a stumble would hurt. The bull case rests on duration: if growth keeps outrunning estimates, as it has historically, the multiple compresses through earnings growth rather than price decline.
  • Copper versus optics. Copper reach shrinks as speeds rise. But Credo isn't a pure copper bet, since it also sells optical DSPs and retimers, and rack-level density increases the number of short-reach links.
  • Competition from Broadcom, Marvell, Astera Labs, and others. They have more scale, and I won't pretend otherwise. But Credo has won on execution, power efficiency, and speed to market in its niche.
  • AI capex cyclicality. A digestion phase would hit CRDO, and that is the largest risk. The counterargument is that networking attach rates rise with cluster size, so connectivity spend can grow faster than compute unit counts.

What would change my mind¶

  • Gross margin falling meaningfully below the mid-60s
  • A top customer cutting orders or shifting its architecture
  • Guidance that decelerates faster than hyperscaler capex does
  • Inventory or receivables growing faster than revenue
