The core story: this is the picks-and-shovels monopoly on the biggest capex cycle in tech history, and the numbers back it up.

PEG of 0.59 says this stock is actually cheap relative to its growth trajectory — you're not overpaying here, you're getting hypergrowth at a value-stock's growth-adjusted multiple.

Margins are the second pillar: 74.8% gross, 66.2% operating, 63.7% net margins, ROE of 117%. This isn't a cyclical hardware commodity business — it's a software-like margin profile bolted onto the hardware layer that every AI workload runs through. That's the moat: CUDA, the ecosystem lock-in, and the fact that hyperscalers and sovereign AI buildouts (like the $3.1B Zankore deal in Indonesia) keep signing multi-billion dollar commitments for NVIDIA silicon specifically, not "a GPU."

Now let's address the bear case in the research directly, because it's weaker than it looks:

  1. "ADX weakening, MACD diverging, MFI fading" — this is short-term technical noise. Weekly and monthly SuperTrend are both firmly bullish with huge cushions (+24.5% and +57.6%). RSI is a neutral 54 — not stretched, not exhausted. What we're seeing is healthy consolidation after an 18% rally, not distribution. You don't panic out of a structural bull trend because ADX dipped below 20 for a few weeks.

  2. Chanos's "GPU rentability" critique — this is a well-worn short-seller narrative that ignores the demand reality: real capital (Zankore's $3.1B loan) is being deployed specifically to buy and deploy NVIDIA GPUs, not rent economics theorizing. Capital doesn't lie — sovereign and hyperscale buyers are voting with tens of billions of dollars.

  3. The AR/inventory "yellow flag" — Sure, receivables grew 127% YoY versus 106% revenue growth. But this is a company scaling shipments as fast as physically possible into a supply-constrained market.
