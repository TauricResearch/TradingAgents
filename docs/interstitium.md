## Overview

In 2018, Neil Theise (NYU Pathology) and Rebecca Wells (U. Penn) published observations of what they called the **interstitium** — a third circulatory system alongside the cardiovascular and lymphatic systems. What were assumed to be isolated interstitial pockets (spaces between and around organs, beneath skin, inside fascia) are actually *connected* — a fluid-filled web of collagen bundles and hyaluronic acid gel, like groundwater flowing through rock.

> "This is clearly a third bodily system for the circulation of fluids." — Rebecca Wells

For 400 years anatomists had catalogued only two bodily transport networks: the lymphatic system (observed 1622) and the cardiovascular system (described 1628).

---

## Anatomy of the Interstitium

| Component | Role |
|-----------|------|
| **Collagen bundles** | Provide tensile strength and structure — the "chicken wire" |
| **Hyaluronic acid gel** | Absorbs and stores water — the spongelike matrix |
| **Fluid, cells, molecules** | Slow flow through the gel, connecting interstitial spaces across organs |

Think of it like groundwater flowing through Earth's crust before returning to surface as springs and rivers — but inside the body, emptying into the lymphatic and cardiovascular systems.

## Acupuncture and the Interstitium

A 2019 cadaver study (Dr. Hongyi Li et al.) injected fluorescent chemical tracers into acupuncture points in hands and feet, then used chest compressions to push fluid through the bodies. Fluorescent photography showed tracers traveling toward the heart within interstitial spaces of the arms and legs — confirming the interstitium as a circulatory route.

A 2021 live-subject replication (15 volunteers, dye injected at forearm acupuncture points) showed the same: dye migrated upward along a route corresponding to the **pericardium meridian** — not through veins or superficial tissue.

> "This pathway doesn't go in the veins, it doesn't go superficially. It goes into the interstitium between the muscles. When I saw that, I said: 'We're onto something. This truly has to do with acupuncture.'" — Andrew Ahn, Harvard Medical School

**Note:** Acupuncture points (mapped by Helene Langevin and Jason Yandow, 2002) lie within the same areas of connective tissue where fluid flows through the interstitium. Traditional Chinese medicine describes chi flowing along 12 meridians — these meridians may correspond to interstitium channels.

## Medical Implications

| Domain | Mechanism |
|--------|-----------|
| **Diabetes** | Specialised interstitium cells around body fat can be recruited to make healthy fat cells — therapeutic target for Type 2 diabetes |
| **IBD / bile ducts** | Immune cells and gut bacteria may migrate from intestines to liver *through* the interstitium — explaining autoimmune bile duct complications in ulcerative colitis |
| **Cancer metastasis** | Tumor cells break down hyaluronic acid to navigate through the interstitium en route to the lymphatic system. Seen in: breast, lung, colon, pancreatic, and skin cancers. |
| **Therapeutic** | Narmafotinib disrupts how tumor cells invade and move through the interstitium — showing promise in early pancreatic cancer trials |

## Evolutionary Significance

- **Hydra** (tiny freshwater invertebrates) have fluid-filled connective tissue called *mesoglea* — structurally analogous to the interstitium, with collagen and a hyaluronic acid-like gel
- **Plants** have the *apoplast* — an analogous interstitial space outside cell membranes that transports water and nutrients
- These analogies suggest fluid moving through interstitial spaces may have been the *first* circulatory system in complex multicellular life, hundreds of millions of years ago
