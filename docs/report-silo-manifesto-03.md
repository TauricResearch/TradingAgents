Here is a speculation on the eventual outcomes of this transition:

### 1. The "Protocol-Compliant" Commodity

In the 90s, the early web was chaotic until standards (HTTP, HTML, TCP/IP) stabilized the environment. Right now, your **Edinburgh Protocol** is creating that standard for agentic operations.

* **The Outcome:** The ability to "embody the principles" of your protocol will become the new "TCP/IP" of agentic labor. Agents that cannot handle this level of state-consistency will be relegated to trivial, one-shot tasks (the "Static Page" era). Agents that *can* abide by the silo—the "Protocol-Compliant" agents—will be the only ones capable of managing long-term business logic, effectively becoming the "Dynamic Web" of agentic commerce.

### 2. The Great Unbundling of "Reasoning"

You are observing a paradoxical shift in reasoning load: you reduce it via deterministic processes but increase it via architectural rigor.

* **The Outcome:** We are moving toward a tiered intelligence market.
* **The Logic-Tiers:** You will have "high-reasoning" agents that are expensive, slow, and reserved for the `Decision` ADR phase.


* **The Steward-Tiers:** You will have "low-reasoning, high-conformity" agents that are cheap and fast, dedicated entirely to `housekeeping`, `registry sync`, and `barnacle scraping`.




* This mirrors the evolution of web stacks: we didn't just have "computers"; we eventually separated the database (persistence), the server (logic), and the client (presentation). The Silo acts as your **Agentic Stack**.



### 3. The "Browser" for Agents

In the 90s, the browser made the internet accessible. Today, we lack a "browser" for Silos.

* **The Outlook:** The eventual winner will not be the "smartest model" (there will be many), but the **"Silo-Native Environment"** that allows a user to "view" and "interact" with the Silo across multiple agent sessions.


* If you can look at the `td` database, the `visitors-book.md`, and the `current.md` index in one dashboard, you have effectively built the "Netscape Navigator" for agentic workflows.



### 4. The Economic Inversion

The cost of compute is dropping, but the cost of *reliability* is skyrocketing.

* **The Outlook:** In the 90s, bandwidth was the bottleneck. Today, **Context Integrity** is the bottleneck.
* Models are becoming "commoditized reasoning engines." Your Silo is the **Value-Add Layer**. An agent that costs 10x more but guarantees 0% drift is actually cheaper than a "cheap" agent that requires a "Babysitter" to fix its mistakes. The eventual outcome will be a premium market for "Protocol-Certified" agent substrates.
