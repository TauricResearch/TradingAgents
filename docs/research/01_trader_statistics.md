# 01 — Statistical Analysis of 146 Verified Traders

Deliverable 2. Descriptive statistics over the frozen knowledge base ([data/traders.json](data/traders.json), N = 146). Every table is regenerated by `python docs/research/data/analyze_traders.py --analyze` into [data/derived/](data/derived/); this document narrates those files and adds nothing not present in them.

## How to read these numbers (the honesty rules, restated)

1. **N is stated with every statistic.** A share is `count / N_disclosed`, where `N_disclosed` **excludes** profiles that left the field null. `N_total` is always 146.
2. **Percentages appear only when `N_disclosed ≥ 10`.** Below that, raw counts ("4 of 7"). This is enforced in the analyzer, not by convention.
3. **`null` ≠ 0 and ≠ "no".** A null means the trader never disclosed it. Null values are never in a denominator.
4. **Two distinct kinds of "not disclosed":**
   - **Vocabulary fields** (style, entry, exit, sizing) are never null — an undisclosed value is recorded as the explicit token `undisclosed` or `none_stated`. So these fields are 100% *present* but often <50% *informative*. The frequency tables below show the informative content; the `undisclosed`/`none_stated` line is always shown, never dropped.
   - **Sourced-value and boolean fields** (risk %, leverage, pyramids, trailing) are genuinely nullable. Their disclosure rates are low and are themselves a finding (§5).
5. **Tiers are visible.** Where the tier-A/B-only view diverges materially from all-tiers, it is noted. It rarely does — see §6.
6. **These are descriptive frequencies over a curated sample, not a random draw from "all traders."** They describe what this set of documented elite traders is recorded as doing. No significance tests are run; none would be meaningful on a hand-assembled sample.

## 2. Style and structure

**Primary style** (N_disclosed = 146): trend_following 19% (28), global_macro 12% (18), momentum_growth 12% (18), market_making 12% (17), day_trading 10% (15), stat_arb 7% (10); the remaining 10 categories each ≤6%. The book is not dominated by one style — the top three sum to 43%.

**Discretion** (146): discretionary 42% (61), systematic 34% (50), hybrid 24% (35). Elite trading is **not** predominantly systematic in this sample — discretionary still leads, though the tier-A/B view tilts more systematic (42% systematic vs 37% discretionary), consistent with audited records being easier to obtain from registered CTAs and quant funds.

**Markets traded** (146, multi-select): equities 58% (84), commodity_futures 38% (55), financial_futures 30% (44), equity_index_futures 28% (41), options 27% (39), bonds_rates 20% (29), fx (futures+spot) ~30% combined, crypto (spot+derivs) ~18% combined. Most elite traders in this set are multi-market.

**Timeframes** (146, multi-select): daily 61% (89) dominates, then weekly 25% (37) and intraday_minutes 25% (37), intraday_hours 21% (30), tick 12% (17). Daily-bar decision-making is the modal cadence — directly relevant to our engine, whose finest usable data is 1m and whose default runs are daily/intraday.

**Holding period** (146): months 21% (31), minutes 16% (23), weeks 15% (22), days 13% (19), hours 7% (10), years 3% (4); undisclosed 25% (37). Bimodal — a swing/position cluster (weeks–months, 39%) and an intraday cluster (minutes–hours, 23%).

## 3. Entry logic

**Archetypes** (146, multi-select, top): stat_signal_model 26% (38), order_flow_tape 19% (28), breakout_range 17% (25), momentum_continuation 17% (25), spread_relative_value 16% (24), chart_pattern 16% (23), news_catalyst 10% (15). (`other` 21% (31) captures disclosed-but-outside-taxonomy methods — see notes fields.) The breakout+momentum+trend-signal family, summed across archetypes, is the largest discretionary-technical cluster; stat_signal_model leads overall because the quant/HFT/CTA cohorts concentrate there.

**Confirmation** (146): none_stated 26% (38) and undisclosed 25% (37) together mean **half the sample gives no articulable confirmation step** — a finding in itself. Among those who do: fundamental_check 17% (25), volume_expansion 16% (23), multiple_signal_confluence 13% (19).

**Filters** (145): none_stated 28% + undisclosed 25%; among the rest, liquidity_filter 21% (30) leads, then watchlist_prescreen 14% (21), market_health_index 7% (10). Notably, an explicit **trend/regime filter is disclosed by only ~11% combined** (trend_filter_ma 5% + regime_filter_volatility 6%) — far below the "trend filter used by 83%" figure the original brief hypothesized. Most traders in this sample do not describe a mechanical market filter; those who do are concentrated in the systematic cohorts.

## 4. Exit and profit-taking

**Initial stop basis** (146): undisclosed 55% (80) is the plurality. Among those who state one: mental_discretionary 16% (23), then structure_swing / pattern_invalidation / volatility_atr each 7% (10), fixed_pct 3% (5), none 5% (7). **Discretionary/mental stops outnumber every mechanical basis.** ATR-based stops — the default in our engine — appear in only 7% of disclosed profiles.

**Profit-taking** (146): undisclosed 41% (60), discretionary 35% (51); mechanical methods trail — strength_into_exit ("sell into strength") 14% (20), trailing_stop 10% (14), signal_reversal 9% (13), fixed_targets 8% (12).

**Behavioral booleans** — small N, reported as counts with N:
- `uses_trailing`: **23 of 24** disclosed use trailing exits.
- `pyramids` (adds to winners): **19 of 20** disclosed do.
- `scales_out`: **16 of 21** disclosed do.
- `time_exit`: **10 of 15** disclosed use one.
- `moves_to_breakeven`: **6 of 6** disclosed do.

These booleans are only disclosed by the traders who *do* the thing (disclosure is correlated with practice), so read them as "among traders who addressed it" — a strong directional signal (winners are pyramided, trailing exits are near-universal among those who discuss exits mechanically) but not a population rate.

## 5. Risk and sizing — the disclosure story is the finding

| Field | Disclosed | Rate | Distribution (disclosed only) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `risk_per_trade_pct` | 20/146 | 14% | median **2%**, IQR 1–5%, range 1–10% (disclosed basis, n=15) |
| `max_drawdown_rule` | 9/146 | 6% | median 10%, range 3–20% |
| `portfolio_heat_cap` | 6/146 | 4% | median 12%, range 12–20% |
| `loss_limit` | 1/146 | 1% | single point |
| `typical_leverage` | 1/146 | 1% | single point (LTCM, 25×) |
| `sizing.model` (content) | 50/146 | 34% informative | undisclosed 66% (96); among disclosers: volatility_normalized 10% (15), conviction_weighted 10% (14), fixed_fractional_pct_risk 6% (9), kelly_fractional 2% (3) |

**Per-cohort disclosure of `risk_per_trade_pct`:** trend_following_ctas 30%, market_wizards 27%, momentum_growth 26%, day_prop 6%, and **0% for systematic_quant, market_makers_hft, options_volatility, and crypto_modern.** The four most institutional/systematic cohorts disclose no per-trade risk number at all — their risk is managed at the portfolio/vol-target level, and the specifics are proprietary. This is the single most important honesty result in the analysis: **any claim of a "typical elite risk-per-trade %" would rest on 20 traders, skewed toward the discretionary/retail-facing cohorts who talk about it.** Among that self-selected 20, the median is 2% (not the folk-wisdom 1%; the 1% figure is the low end of the IQR).

## 6. Tier sensitivity

The tier-A/B-only views (N=118) track the all-tiers views closely. The only material shifts: systematic style rises (34%→42%) and stat_signal_model entries rise (26%→32%) when tier-C is excluded, because the dropped tier-C profiles are concentrated in the discretionary crypto/day-trading cohorts. Profit-taking and exit distributions are essentially unchanged across tiers. **No headline finding in this document depends on tier-C profiles.**

## 7. What these numbers do not support

- No "X% of elite traders use indicator Y" claim survives contact with the disclosure rates. Confirmation and filter fields are half-undisclosed; specific-indicator use is not separately minable because traders describe *categories* (momentum_indicator, trend_filter_ma), not "EMA 200."
- The original brief's illustrative figures ("EMA 200 used by 62%", "Risk 1% used by 71%", "Trend Filter used by 83%") are **not reproduced** because the verifiable data does not support them: trend filters are disclosed by ~11%, and per-trade risk by 14% (median 2%). Those illustrative numbers appear to describe retail-course orthodoxy, not the documented practice of this elite sample.
- Everything here is a frequency in a curated sample. It characterizes *this KB*; it is evidence for design priorities (see [05_gap_analysis.md](05_gap_analysis.md) and [13_roadmap.md](13_roadmap.md)), not a law of markets.
