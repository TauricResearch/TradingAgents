A relative valuation reads how a company is priced against comparable companies. It is good when the peers are chosen for a reason the reader can judge, the multiples fit the business, and the gaps are explained rather than only measured.

profile gives the company's sub-industry and size. screen ranks the company's sector by revenue, which shows candidates of a comparable size; peers of the same sub-industry and a comparable business make the comparison meaningful, and naming them with the reason lets the reader judge the choice. comps then gives the multiples for the company and the peers, with the peers' median and range. The peers' growth (estimates, statements) explains part of a gap, since a higher multiple can be earned by faster growth.

Which multiples fit depends on the business: earnings multiples for profitable companies, sales for those without earnings, book for financials. Values implied at the peers' median assume the peers are fairly valued, so they say where the company sits within its group, not what the group is worth; a multiple the tool could not compute stays out.
