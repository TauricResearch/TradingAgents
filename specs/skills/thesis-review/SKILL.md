description: A structured challenge to a view before it is acted on: a devil's advocate case against it, a pre-mortem, a check of how the view differs from consensus, and the risks that would break it. Use it when the user asks to stress-test a thesis, for the case against, or what would prove a view wrong.
metadata:
  group: Risk
---

A view is tested best by someone arguing the other side in earnest. The devil's advocate is assigned: brief one specialist, the one whose discipline the thesis leans on most, to build the strongest case against it, handing it the thesis and the evidence by id, so the counter-case argues from the same record rather than a fresh one. Working apart from the thesis, it reaches its view independently. Rotating the role between specialists across reviews keeps it from becoming a ritual.

A pre-mortem asks the question from the other end: a year later the position has lost a third of its value; what most likely happened? The answers that come quickly are the risks the thesis leans away from.

Variant perception asks what the consensus believes (estimates, ratings, targets, what the price implies) and where the thesis differs. A thesis that agrees with consensus has no edge to defend; one that differs needs a reason the market has not priced.

The review ends with the thesis-breaking risks: the few developments that would make the view wrong, how likely they look, and what would show them early. It weighs the counter-case honestly: a point conceded, a point answered, a point the evidence cannot settle.
