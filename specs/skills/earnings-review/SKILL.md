An earnings review reads what a company's latest filed quarter showed and why. It is good when the numbers and their drivers come together: the change against the year before, and management's own account of what moved it.

filings finds the latest 10-Q or 10-K and its period. statements gives that period's income statement and cash flow (quarterly; annual for a 10-K) with the year-earlier period: revenue, gross and operating margin, diluted EPS, operating cash flow, capital expenditure and free cash flow, with changes and margins worked out through calc. The management discussion (mdna) of that filing, read with filing_section part by part, gives what drove the results, guidance and the risks management names, best cited in its own words. estimates gives the current consensus and the next date, and news how the results were received.

The data has no record of the consensus before the report, so whether the quarter beat or missed comes from a news result that states it, cited. How the disclosed risks and their wording changed is a filing review's subject (filing-review).
