A macro outlook reads the economy and what markets expect of it: growth and labor, inflation, policy, rates and the curve, credit and volatility. It is good when each figure carries its period and when it became known, since economic data arrives after the period it describes, and when market-implied expectations sit beside the data they anticipate.

markets gives the overview in one step: macro (latest indicators and their changes), yield_curve, central_banks, rate_indicators (fed funds, SOFR, MOVE), tips_breakevens, credit_spreads and volatility. macro_series gives the series the question turns on (CPI and core CPI, the unemployment rate, payrolls, GDP), with the change from a year earlier; the 2s10s spread is exact through calc. For policy expectations, prediction_markets gives the odds on the next central bank decision, and news (topic fed, impact high) what officials said.

Market-implied odds and breakevens are prices, which is how they read best: what the market pays, not a forecast. The outlook itself is an inference from the data and those prices, marked as one, with the releases ahead that would confirm or change it.
